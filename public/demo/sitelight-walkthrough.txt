SiteLight guided walkthrough

00:00 Why SiteLight?
Compare lunar sites and mission dates in one workspace. See sunlight and Earth visibility.
Useful when a schedule changes and you need to reassess a site.

00:08 Two independent scenarios
Mission A and B hold separate sites and start times. Begin by selecting B, the Nobile study point.
Use the same requirements to compare choices fairly.

00:18 Set mission requirements
Choose the site, UTC start and duration. Here we require 70% sunlight, 90% Earth visibility and 70% overlap.
Apply settings evaluates the draft before replacing the current result.

00:40 Moon and terrain views
Moon shows the wider location. Terrain offers a cinematic view and a separate measured Source DEM.
Use Source DEM for the selected elevation grid. Cinematic landforms are illustrative.

00:59 Horizon and time lanes
The horizon plot places the Sun and Earth against surrounding terrain. Scrub time or play the timeline.
The Sun, Earth and Overlap lanes show when the selected conditions are available.

01:11 Compare the actual results
Compare keeps both scenarios under shared requirements. Read the sunlight, Earth and overlap values side by side.
For this setup: A sunlight 0%. B sunlight 73.1%, Earth 100%, overlap 73.1%.

01:33 Test a different start
Schedule scans loaded records for this site and these requirements. Preview a tested start, then apply it.
Preview keeps the baseline unchanged. Apply tested start rechecks it before updating the mission.

01:47 Inspect the ground
Terrain shows slope, roughness and coverage. Switch to Source DEM for measured elevation.
A footprint or sampled route provides screening evidence. Landing safety needs further assessment.

01:57 Replay Apollo evidence
Seismic adds historical catalogs and real archived impact waveforms. Replay the record and inspect its source.
Normalized waveforms explain historical observations. They do not predict local shaking or future events.

02:09 Thermal and dust context
View historical Diviner observations. Update sensitivity to explore assumed dust coverage.
Historical data provide context. Dust sensitivity remains an exploratory model.

02:20 Logistics trade-offs
Explore assumed ice and budget inputs beside a published NASA water-ISRU reference.
Ice and equipment choices are hypothetical. The reference study stays separate.

02:30 Trace and export evidence
Evidence links layers to their sources, validation and limits. Export keeps settings, results and qualifications together.
Use JSON for full evidence, CSV for time records, or Print / PDF for a review copy.

02:46 Your next comparison
Start another comparison in Explore. Set your requirements, compare the results and inspect the evidence.
Prepared real records. On-demand science needs a backend. Scientific acceptance remains open.
