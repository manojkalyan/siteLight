# Explore celestial guides and playback

Local preview only. This increment has not been committed, pushed or deployed.

The public landing state selects scenario B (the real Nobile study point) in cinematic Terrain view; scenario A retains Shackleton for comparison. `?scenario=A` can select the other scenario explicitly. The mission timeline starts automatically at four mission-hours per playback second, loops over the selected mission duration, and offers a visible Click to pause / Click to play hint. Scrubbing or stepping pauses intentionally and remains paused across tabs. Automatic playback resumes after returning to Explore or from background/settings when it was not manually paused. Reduced-motion preference disables automatic playback. Playback affects only the displayed mission time, not scenario inputs or assessment totals; display updates are capped at approximately twenty per second.

Open Explore, select a scenario, switch to Terrain, and move the mission timeline. A compact toolbar gives plain-language Sun/Earth status. Find Sun and Find Earth reframe the camera to the selected direction; the information button reveals angles, model context and the solar-elevation distinction. The cinematic and Source DEM views include enlarged Sun/Earth direction symbols, incoming gold sunlight rays and a blue Earth sightline. Light effects hides the illustrative objects and returns to inspection lighting. The toolbar remains available. Find Sun/Earth turns effects back on when needed.

Scene labels stay attached to their actual projected markers. If a marker is outside the view or under a UI card, its floating label is hidden rather than placed in an unrelated position. The toolbar remains available to locate it. The toolbar itself avoids the mission, result, horizon and timeline cards. Its controls use native keyboard-accessible buttons, focus indicators and an announced locate action.

`readViewport()` obtains the instantaneous record from the existing `visibilityRecord()` evaluator. The same provider geometry, terrain horizon and solar-elevation requirement drive the timeline and the new cues. Rays appear when `lit` is true, independently of whether the selected minimum solar elevation is met. Equality with the terrain horizon remains blocked. The blue sightline requires `earth` to be true. Unknown geometry/coverage never produces visible rays.

The light direction follows local azimuth/elevation. The physical Sun light switches off when the local model blocks sunlight. A separate, shadow-free, oblique inspection light plus ambient fill keeps crater relief readable at every selected site, including with effects disabled. The inspection light follows the viewing angle, has no effect on lunar visibility calculations, and is disabled in Moon view and preview renders. A visible caption identifies inspection lighting when sunlight is blocked or unavailable. Geometry is illustrative in cinematic mode. Neither the artistic scene nor its renderer shadows replace the scientific terrain-horizon calculation. Rays are explanatory lines and do not calculate irradiance, array output, energy storage or a radio link budget. Symbols use arbitrary enlarged display sizes/distances. No new external data or texture is required.

The Sun/Earth effects are confined to Explore's terrain scene. The Moon overview now shows the selected site name and rounded latitude/longitude with a source-attached blue marker and a pointer that avoids cards and viewport edges. Imagery credit and the lighting explanation are available under Scene info. Lunar marker coordinates, terrain screening, source data, scientific inputs and assessment/export values remain unchanged. The guide's objects share the existing renderer and dispose with it. Preview renders hide the direction objects.

Verification: production build; Chrome checks against lit and blocked records from the real Nobile study point; source DEM and cinematic toggles; guide toggle; unchanged A/B scenarios and requirements; Screening isolation; viewport bounds from mobile to desktop; existing deployment/report/media contracts. Review captures remain in the ignored `.deploy-local/celestial` directory.

Terrain layers starts with Footprint and Slope cells selected; both remain optional user controls. The Mission collection popover uses spaced cards, scroll gutters and an opaque background, with the data-mode selector in a separate footer. Generated reports, screenshots and scratch files are excluded from publication.
